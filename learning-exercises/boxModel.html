What you'll learn: How content, padding, border, and margin affect an element's size and spacing.

Try it: Change padding and margin separately and observe which space changes.

Detailed explanation: The CSS box model

Every element is laid out as a box. From inside to outside, its layers are content, padding, border, and margin. Padding is inside the border; margin is outside it. By default, width describes the content area, so padding and borders increase the rendered size. The border-box sizing rule makes declared width include padding and borders.

Example: Increase padding to make the colored area around the text larger. Increase margin to move neighboring boxes farther away.

Common pitfall: Margin is transparent space outside the border. Padding is part of the element's background area, so do not use the two terms interchangeably.
