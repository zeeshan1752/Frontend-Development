Detailed explanation: CSS positioning

Static is the default flow. Relative positioning offsets an element from its normal position while preserving its original space. Absolute positioning removes an element from normal flow and places it relative to its nearest positioned ancestor. Fixed positioning is relative to the viewport. Sticky positioning follows normal flow until it reaches a scroll offset within its container.

Example: Give a parent position: relative and a child position: absolute to anchor the child in that parent. A sticky bar needs scrollable space within its containing region.

Common pitfall: An absolutely positioned element may cover other content because it no longer reserves space. Sticky positioning is constrained by its scroll container and containing block.
